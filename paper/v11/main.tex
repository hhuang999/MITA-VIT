\begin{abstract}
Deep-layer ViT attention maps can emphasise background. We test reading changes with frozen models, using the fast-weight view to link image specificity, spatial correspondence and output contribution to diagnostics, corrections and matched controls. On DeiT-S/B, norm weighting raises object enrichment by $0.038/0.065$ and image-specific human alignment by $0.033/0.058$; it beats its control in three of four comparisons. Deletion-score and DINO gains remain below registered margins. Probe relocation lowers all nine deep-layer model--metric scores; the combination improves only one of four registered supervised comparisons. Norm weighting thus provides bounded alignment benefits, while declining index decodability alone does not justify relocation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Attention maps visualise what a vision transformer (ViT)~\citep{dosovitskiy2021} attends to~\citep{caron2021,abnar2020,chefer2021}. Deep-layer maps can emphasise high-norm background tokens; registers address this by changing the model~\citep{darcet2024,jiang2025}. We ask how much map quality improves by changing only the \emph{reading rule}.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\rsub{Reading attention and evaluating maps.} Whether attention explains predictions is debated~\citep{jain2019,wiegreffe2019}. Readings use layer aggregation~\citep{abnar2020}, relevance propagation~\citep{chefer2021}, gradients~\citep{chefer2021generic}, norms~\citep{kobayashi2020,zhu2024} or filtering~\citep{ayyar2025}. Output weighting also underlies CAM~\citep{zhou2016}; norm-based attention analysis includes transformed-value magnitudes~\citep{kobayashi2020}. GlobEnc and ALTI incorporate components beyond attention and aggregate token interactions across layers~\citep{modarressi2022,ferrando2022}; we isolate a local, class-agnostic reading. Parameter sensitivity~\citep{adebayo2018}, occlusion choices~\citep{bluecher2024} and spatial priors affect evaluation. CNN centre-bias findings~\citep{kuang2026} motivate controls, without establishing the same mechanism in ViTs.

\rsub{Deep tokens and fast weights.} Registers address high-norm background tokens~\citep{darcet2024,jiang2025}; no-op heads provide a related functional analogy~\citep{bondarenko2023}, and supervision shapes attention~\citep{walmer2023}. Input-token identity remains largely decodable but declines with depth in a language transformer~\citep{brunner2020}; deep CLS-trained ViT tokens retain input-location information under representational-similarity analysis~\citep{raghu2021}. Fast weights are written by another network~\citep{schmidhuber1992}. This view connects linear attention to fast-weight programming~\citep{schlag2021} and softmax attention to Hopfield memory~\citep{ramsauer2021}, and motivates MiTA~\citep{wen2026mita}. We use it to organise empirical tests, rather than introduce an attention mechanism.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\rsub{An exact representation, an interpretive framework.} In layer $\ell$, normalised tokens $x_1,\dots,x_{N+1}$ (CLS and $N=196$ patches) enter $H$ heads with $d$-dimensional queries/keys. Affine projections form $q_i^h,k_j^h,v_j^h$ (App.~\ref{app:setup}). The attention-branch output before residual addition is
\begin{equation}
o_i=b_O+\sum_{h,j}a_{ij}^h W_O^h v_j^h,\qquad
a_{ij}^h=\operatorname{softmax}_j\!\big(q_i^{h\top}k_j^h/\sqrt d\big).
\label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
For a fixed image, head $h$ is a two-layer network in $q_i^h$: unit $j$ has input weights $k_j^h/\sqrt d$ and output weights $W_O^h v_j^h$. Softmax couples activations; dropout is inactive at evaluation. This representation is exact; reading assumptions require empirical tests. Received attention is $s_j=\sum_{h,i}a_{ij}^h$, placed at patch $j$. Queries include all tokens; maps exclude CLS and sum layers 9--12, fixed before formal runs.

\rsub{Assumptions, proxies and controls.} Figure~\ref{fig:diag} tests image specificity, spatial correspondence and output contribution. Mean-map structure, index decodability and high-norm attention (a--c) motivate corrections (d--f). Unit meaning requires measurement~\citep{bau2017}, and activation omits transformed-value magnitudes~\citep{kobayashi2020}. An assumption-specific benefit requires improvement over R0 \emph{and} its control; diagnostics remain proxies.

Mean subtraction (C1) uses $s-\bar s$, with held-out mean $\bar s$; its control subtracts a centred Gaussian. Relocation (C2) uses $r_p=\sum_j s_jP(p\mid x_j)$, decoding index rather than content provenance; its control matches mean squared token displacement. Norm weighting (C3) uses $c_j=\sum_{h,i}a_{ij}^h\lVert W_O^hv_j^h\rVert_2$, omitting cancellation and later computation; its control zeros received attention at high-norm patches. Aggregation and control calibration are detailed in App.~\ref{app:setup}.

\rsub{Metrics and decision rules.} A centred Gaussian correlates $0.41$ with raw ClickMe maps. We define two \emph{centre-controlled} metrics: \emph{same-ring enrichment}, the object rate of top-scoring patches minus the rate at the same radius, using ImageNet-S masks~\citep{gao2023}; and \emph{image-specific alignment}, own-image minus other-image Spearman correlation with ClickMe human maps~\citep{linsley2019,fel2022}. These controls target specific prior effects. Our \emph{deletion score} adapts the LIF--MIF area difference~\citep{bluecher2024} to top-1 preservation under token removal. With 1,000 paired image-bootstrap resamples, a difference is \emph{higher/lower} if its 95\% interval excludes zero and its magnitude reaches $0.02$ ($0.05$ for deletion); otherwise it is \emph{comparable}, a registered category, not equivalence.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
\rsub{Setup and registered predictions.} We evaluate frozen DeiT-S/B and DINO ViT-S/16. Model-side comparisons use a test-time register~\citep{jiang2025} and released MiTA checkpoint~\citep{wen2026mita}, both at size S. Data: 5,000 ImageNet images~\citep{deng2009}, 12,419 ImageNet-S images, 17,408 ClickMe maps and 4,000 held-out images (2,000 probe-train; 2,000 calibration/test). The protocol was frozen before formal runs, after smoke tests and earlier observations (App.~\ref{app:setup}). \textbf{R1:} DINO norm weighting and early relocation remain comparable. \textbf{R2:} the combination improves all four supervised alignment comparisons. \textbf{R3:} neither model-side variant is higher than DeiT-S. Combination--R0 and correction--control contrasts are primary; other correction--R0 contrasts are descriptive, except R1's registered negative controls. Diagnosis--benefit interpretation is post hoc.

\rsub{Execution and sanity checks.} With $k=16$ region-selected units, prediction agreement is $72/83/61\%$ in DeiT-S/B/DINO, versus $9/30/4\%$ for random units (App.~\ref{app:more}). All five readings pass the stored parameter-randomisation criterion (App.~\ref{app:sanity}).

\rsub{Diagnostic patterns (Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}a--c).} In layers 6--12, mean maps explain $3$--$6\%$ of spatial variance in DeiT-S/B and at most $17\%$ in DINO; their deep-layer structure is $82$--$98\%$ radial. Index accuracy falls from nearly $100\%$ to $18/32/36\%$ at layer 12 (chance: $1/196$). High-norm patches (block-input norm $>3$ times the image median) receive up to $9/44\%$ of attention in DeiT-S/B. DINO's share is at most $0.025\%$, zero in layers 9--12 on ImageNet-S. Supporting uniform-crop analysis gives high-norm value-norm ratios of $0.29/0.23$ (App.~\ref{app:more}). Proxies alone do not establish misreading.

\rsub{Correction outcomes (Table~\ref{tab:main}; Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}d--f).}
\textbf{Norm weighting improves alignment, with limits.} Enrichment rises by $0.038/0.065$ and ClickMe by $0.033/0.058$ in DeiT-S/B. C3 also beats its control in three of four comparisons; DeiT-B ClickMe is comparable. Deletion gains ($0.017/0.029$) remain below $0.05$. DINO changes are at most $0.004$, satisfying this part of R1. Post hoc, assigned weights outperform shuffled weights in all four supervised alignment comparisons; value-only weighting is comparable (App.~\ref{app:more}). C3 aggregates established norm-weighted components~\citep{kobayashi2020,zhu2024}.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Other corrections lack consistent benefits.} Mean subtraction's only R0 gain (DINO ClickMe) is comparable to its control. Relocation lowers all nine deep-layer comparisons and underperforms matched blur in DeiT-S/DINO. Early relocation remains comparable (R1). R2 \emph{partly holds}: one supervised comparison is higher, one comparable and two lower.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Model-side results have a narrower scope.} Register gains ($0.012/0.013$) are below $0.02$. MiTA scores lower but differs in training/preprocessing. R3 holds under the registered comparisons, without establishing a general advantage over model-side interventions.

\rsub{Does diagnosis predict correction benefit?} The contribution pattern is consistent with its interpretation, but dose--gain associations are weak (Spearman $0.045$--$0.158$; App.~\ref{app:more}). Local-error and MLP checks narrow relocation explanations (App.~\ref{app:reloc}); capacity/calibration limitations remain possible. Index decoding differs from content provenance. Prior ViT representational-similarity evidence~\citep{raghu2021} does not test our probe or explain this failure; the cause remains unresolved.

\section{Discussion}
\rsub{Interpretation and limitations.} Reading changes can improve alignment without deletion gains reaching the margin; declining index decodability does not by itself justify relocation. Evidence covers three frozen S/B classifiers and received attention. CLS maps, larger/register-trained models, repeated training seeds and high-norm threshold sensitivity are untested. ClickMe may overlap model training data. Gains are modest, visually subtle (App.~\ref{app:gallery}) and below GAE's descriptive scores. General diagnostic prediction remains open; image-bootstrap intervals condition on fixed checkpoints, probes and pairing.

\rsub{Ethical considerations.} Public images and checkpoints contain people and known biases. Better map alignment remains partial evidence about model behaviour.

\section{Conclusion}
Norm weighting improves deep-layer alignment in DeiT-S/B; index-probe relocation fails, and the registered combination lacks consistent gains. The fast-weight view organises these controlled tests. Broader diagnostic validation requires token-to-input attribution and model coverage, while separating alignment from deletion behaviour.
