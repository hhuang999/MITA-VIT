# Primary-source context for geometry and evaluation claims

Checked 2026-11-13 against author/venue pages or publisher-indexed records. The following are scientific scope notes, not claims that these papers established the present C3 mechanism.

| Source and verified metadata | What it supports here | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Gao et al., “Large-Scale Unsupervised Semantic Segmentation,” *IEEE TPAMI* 45(6):7457–7476, 2023, doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2022.3218275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36315550/) | ImageNet-S benchmark provenance; v12 entry matches authors, volume, pages and DOI. | The present mask-crop pairing and enrichment metric are this repository's implementation, not a published ImageNet-S metric. |
| [Touvron et al., “Training data-efficient image transformers & distillation through attention,” *ICML/PMLR* 139:10347–10357, 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/touvron21a.html) | DeiT model provenance. | Does not attribute any C3 gain to a specific trained feature. |
| [Caron et al., “Emerging Properties in Self-Supervised Vision Transformers,” *ICCV* 2021:9650–9660](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Caron_Emerging_Properties_in_Self-Supervised_Vision_Transformers_ICCV_2021_paper.html) | DINO provenance and authors' observation that self-supervised ViT features contain salient segmentation information. | Different supervision, checkpoint, crop and baseline quality complicate causal interpretation of our DINO–DeiT difference. |
| [Raghu et al., “Do Vision Transformers See Like Convolutional Neural Networks?”, *NeurIPS* 34, 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/652cf38361a209088302ba2b8b7f51e0-Abstract.html) | ViT representations preserve spatial information, but this does not establish that a token's map slot identifies the originating pixels. Existing v12 citation is appropriate. | It is a representational analysis, not an attribution validity test for C3. |
| [Walmer et al., “Teaching Matters: Investigating the Role of Supervision in Vision Transformers,” *CVPR* 2023:7486–7496](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Walmer_Teaching_Matters_Investigating_the_Role_of_Supervision_in_Vision_Transformers_CVPR_2023_paper.html) | Training regime can change ViT attention and downstream behavior, supporting cautious model-specific interpretation. | Does not predict the C3 difference. |
| [Blücher, Vielhaben and Strodthoff, “Decoupling Pixel Flipping and Occlusion Strategy for Consistent XAI Benchmarks,” *TMLR*, June 2024](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=bIiLXdtUVM) | Deletion/pixel-flipping rankings depend on the occlusion strategy; the paper proposes symmetric relevance gain (SRG). Existing v12 citation is directly relevant. | Our implementation adapts LIF−MIF to top-1 preservation; do not claim it is the authors' exact score or occlusion-robust without testing other occlusions. |
| [Adebayo et al., “Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps,” *NeurIPS*, 2018](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/hash/294a8ed24b1ad22ec2e7efea049b8737-Abstract.html) | Parameter/data randomization tests motivate avoiding visual plausibility as sole evidence. | Their findings do not automatically apply to C3. |
| [Kuang, Carmichael and Purdom, “When Random Saliency Looks Trained: Architectural Center Bias in CNN Interpretability,” *ICML/PMLR* 306:61236–61266, 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/kuang26a.html) | Architecture-generated centre bias is a concrete confound in CNN saliency; motivates an explicit centre control. | The paper reports this bias largely absent in the ViTs studied. It must not be cited as proof that ViT C3's behaviour is centre bias. Existing v12 wording correctly distinguishes this. |
| [Bylinskii et al., “What Do Different Evaluation Metrics Tell Us About Saliency Models?”, *IEEE TPAMI* 41(3):740–757, 2019, doi:10.1109/TPAMI.2018.2815601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29993800/) | Original systematic demonstration that saliency metrics weight false positives, spatial deviation and biases differently, supporting endpoint-aware interpretation of our enrichment/AUROC divergence. | Their task is human fixation prediction, whereas ours uses class segmentation; cite only the general metric-design lesson, preferably in appendix if space permits. |
| [Jelassi, Sander and Li, “Vision Transformers Provably Learn Spatial Structure,” *NeurIPS* 35, 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/f69707de866eb0805683d3521756b73f-Paper-Conference.pdf) | Shows a positional-attention mechanism can learn spatial structure in a simplified setting; useful if a deeper related-work paragraph discusses learned spatial priors. | Theory is for a simplified model/dataset; it cannot explain C3 gains or the present quartile pattern. The proceedings HTML abstract contains inconsistent “delocalized” wording; use the paper PDF for substantive interpretation. |

For the immediate v13 geometry paragraph, existing `gao2023`, `raghu2021`, `walmer2023`, `bluecher2024`, and `kuang2026` references are enough. The strongest *new* optional citation is Bylinskii et al. for the general principle that evaluation endpoints can disagree. The mathematical all-foreground zero and the AUROC/enrichment divergence should be supported directly by this repository's source and results, rather than attributed to external papers.
